Behold a rare surviving artifact: a postscript, hand-inked, extracted from a letter sent across a distance of state lines by myself, then nineteen years of age, to my younger brother, aged nine. I had departed the family home abruptly in 1990, drawn by the migratory pull to prestigious university education.

---

![letter to brother](/posts/letter-to-brother-1992/letter-to-brother-1992.webp)

---

*"P.S. Noah — study my handwriting. There is much meaning there left unsaid. Aspire to have scratches as expressive as mine and you will surpass my skill."*

Here we witness rendered in ink: the illegible scrawl, presented not as a deficiency to be corrected but as evidence of some interior richness so vast that ordinary penmanship cannot contain it. "There is much meaning there left unsaid," I wrote, of my own handwriting, essentially inviting the reader to marvel at a depth that I declined to unpack. A weaker performer, aware of his sloppy handwriting, might simply try harder next time. Instead I elevated the flaw into a standard as a challenge for my brother to chase.

What we are able to document here, then, is not merely handwriting but my *wit*, preserved by chance across thirty-four years. This is evidence that as early as 1992, I possessed sufficient irony to turn a legibility complaint into a small, self-satisfied joke at my own expense.
